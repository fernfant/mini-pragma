Step 4 — Output: the guess blooms

The firing pattern becomes a score for every candidate word. Softmax turns those scores into percentages, and the model commits to the brightest one.

overview Whole flow step 1 Encode step 2 Attention step 3 Neurons step 4 Output

We have a firing pattern — the model's thought about "once upon a". The last step turns that thought into an actual word. One more weighted sum — one dot-product per candidate word, exactly like the neuron layer did, just producing 5 numbers instead of 10 — gives each candidate a raw score (called a logit, just an unbounded number): higher means "this fits better". Then softmax — the same squasher from attention — turns the scores into probabilities that add to 100%. The tallest bar wins.

Predict Before the reveal

Softmax Scores → confidence

The firing neurons feed one last weighted sum, producing a raw score per word. For "once upon a" the scores come out wildly uneven — and softmax exaggerates the gap, sending almost all the probability to the top word:

raw scores (logits):  time 8.3   blue -3.9   you -0.5   much -0.2   cake -4.1
softmax →             time 100%  blue  0%   you  0%   much  0%   cake  0%

predicted next word →  "time"

(Those percentages are rounded — "time" is really ~99.97%, not a literal 100%, and the others are tiny but never exactly 0.)

That's the entire forward pass: once upon a → time. To write a whole sentence, a model just does this again and again — tack the chosen word onto the end and run the pass once more for the next one. Notice softmax shows up twice in the pipeline: once to mix attention, once to pick the winner. Same tool, two jobs — turning a row of numbers into a set of choices that add to 100%.

Drag a logit. Push "blue"'s raw score up and watch softmax steal probability away from "time" — a small change in the score makes a big change in the odds:

Honest A real guess, from a real net

Every number on these four pages was computed, not faked: this tiny network was actually trained (same predict → measure error → nudge loop as Lesson 1) so each phrase lands on its true next word — once upon a → time, the sky is → blue. It has a 5-word vocabulary, not GPT's 50,000, but the machinery is identical. You've now traced a full forward pass end to end.

✅ You can now say…
  • The output layer gives each candidate word a raw score (logit).
  • Softmax turns the scores into probabilities that add to 100%, exaggerating the gaps.
  • The model picks the highest — and writes longer text by repeating the whole pass one word at a time.
