Lesson 5c — Watch it think

Every piece you've built — embeddings, attention, the feed-forward net — wired into one machine. Watch a single thought flow left to right, or grab the attention dials and become the model.

← L5 putting it together 🎯 L3 attention

You've met every part separately: a word becomes a vector of numbers (L2), attention lets each word gather meaning from the others (L3), and a little feed-forward net of neurons does the thinking (L1.5, L4). Here they're all wired together, running on three words — "the cat sat" — as the model guesses the next word. Press play and watch one thought travel through.

Flow The forward pass, on one screen

Hit ▶ Play for the full sweep, ⏭ Step to walk it one stage at a time, or drag the attention dials to set the blend yourself and watch the whole chain recompute.

Seeing What each stage is doing

  • Words come in. Three tokens, with the last one ("sat") ringed — that's the focus word doing the predicting.
  • They become numbers. Each word lights up as its embedding: a short column of values, colour showing sign and size.
  • Attention routes meaning. The focus word pulls a weighted mix of every word into one blend. The percentages are a real softmax over the word-to-word scores.
  • Neurons fire. The blend runs through a layer of neurons; ReLU zeroes the ones that don't activate, leaving a sparse pattern — the "thinking".
  • The answer blooms. That pattern becomes scores over candidate next-words; softmax squashes them to add to 100%, and the model commits to the brightest.
Why "illustrative"? The embeddings and attention weights are computed for real, so the flow is honest. The neuron and output weights are fixed random numbers — enough to show the mechanism, not a trained model. Don't read meaning into which word wins; read meaning into how changing the blend changes the firing and the guess.
✅ You can now say…
  • A forward pass is one left-to-right trip: encode → attention → feed-forward → output.
  • Attention builds a weighted blend of the words; change the weights and you change what the rest of the network sees.
  • The output is a probability distribution over next words — the model picks the highest.
